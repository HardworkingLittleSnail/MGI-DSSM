SG-DiTs: A physics-informed diffusion-transformer hybrid framework for
reliable battery RUL prediction
Jing Sun a,* , Jingfan Gao a
a
    School of Information and Electronic Engineering, Shandong Technology and Business University, Yantai, 264005, China




A R T I C L E I N F O                                        A B S T R A C T

Keywords:                                                    Accurate Remaining Useful Life (RUL) prediction for lithium-ion batteries is critical for system safety but remains
Lithium-ion batteries (LIBs)                                 challenged by the inherent stochasticity of degradation trajectories and pervasive operational noise. While
Remaining Useful Life (RUL)                                  existing data-driven methods primarily treat RUL prediction as a deterministic regression task, they often
Diffusion-transformer (DiTs)
                                                             struggle to quantify prediction uncertainty and recover underlying aging trends from highly fluctuating data. To
Multidimensional feature extraction
                                                             bridge this gap, this study proposes Savitzky-Golay Diffusion Transformer (SG-DiTs), a novel physics-informed
Savitzky-Golay filtering (SG)
                                                             generative framework that integrates SG filtering with a hybrid Diffusion-Transformer network. Unlike tradi­
                                                             tional approaches, the SG-DiTs framework reformulates prediction as a probabilistic generative process: it learns
                                                             to reconstruct the pristine degradation trajectory from a Gaussian noise distribution conditioned on historical
                                                             health indicators. Specifically, SG filtering is employed to stabilize the extraction of multidimensional featur­
                                                             es—including Incremental Capacity Analysis (ICA), Shannon Entropy (SE), and Maximum Lyapunov Exponent
                                                             (MLE)—has enhanced prediction accuracy. The Transformer backbone captures long-term temporal de­
                                                             pendencies, while the diffusion mechanism models the aleatoric uncertainty of the aging process. Experimental
                                                             validation on Toyota and CALCE datasets demonstrate that SG-DiTs significantly outperforms both deterministic
                                                             baselines (CNN-LSTM, CNN-Trans, Transformer) and probabilistic benchmarks Gaussian Process Regression
                                                             (GPR). By explicitly modeling degradation distributions, this method reduces the root mean square error (RMSE)
                                                             by at least 42% and 70%, respectively, while demonstrating improved accuracy through trajectory reconstruc­
                                                             tion, exceptional robustness, and interpretability.
